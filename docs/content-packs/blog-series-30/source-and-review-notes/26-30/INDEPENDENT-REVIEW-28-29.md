# Independent source and editorial review — Blogs 28–29

Read-only comparison of the complete draft JSON articles with the actual supplied textbook passages and reopened primary publication records. No clinical article was rewritten by this review. No substantive clinical correction identified.

For blog 28, Henry’s Clinical Diagnosis and Management by Laboratory Methods, 24th edition (2022), chapter 23, PDF pages 385 and 389, and Tietz Fundamentals of Clinical Chemistry and Molecular Diagnostics, ninth edition (2024), chapter 38, PDF pages 2062–2063 support the stool-antigen/antibody distinction and selected initial/follow-up use. Harrison’s Principles of Internal Medicine, 22nd edition (2025), chapter 168, PDF page 1344 was read in full and explicitly supports the medication and treatment-timing context for stool antigen testing. The draft does not transfer a numerical washout interval, fixed fasting period, treatment regimen or universal endoscopy age from the textbooks into patient instructions. It advises disclosure of medicines and prohibits independent changes. Persistent symptoms and alarm features remain clinical questions; a negative stool result is not presented as exclusion of every gastrointestinal cause. Stool handling is distinguished from routine home blood collection.

The PubMed record for Das and colleagues, 31 May 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.ijmmb.2025.100888, was reopened. It is a letter without an abstract, and the article appropriately uses only the existence of this professional publication as its hook. No unreviewed clinical conclusion is attributed to that letter.

For blog 29, relevant actual passages were reread in Henry’s chapter 42, PDF pages 990 and 994–996, plus Laposata’s Clinical Diagnostic Tests (2016), chapter 2, PDF pages 105–106, 109 and 111. The distinction between a routine coagulation assessment and selected inherited/acquired thrombophilia investigations is sound. The draft does not imply a universal risk panel or certain future event. Acute thrombosis, pregnancy, acquired changes and anticoagulant assay interference are appropriately qualified, and patients are explicitly told not to stop prescribed anticoagulants independently. No historical testing-age rule, numerical threshold, medication interruption schedule or MTHFR screening recommendation is adopted. Acute clot assessment and long-term risk investigation remain separate tasks.

The Journal of Hematology and Allied Sciences primary study page was reopened: Kaler and colleagues, DOI 10.25259/JHAS_51_2025, published 30 July 2026 (online ahead of print 25 April 2026). The article confines its hook to a selected Mumbai genetic-referral cohort and explicitly does not adopt the paper’s panel as a general screening guideline or QXL specification.

Both articles preserve the fictional-composite label, named assigned reviewer, exact unavailable-information wording, owner-supplied Same Day reporting and emergency-care boundaries. No local stool-antigen, coagulation or genetic method is inferred from the general Roche/ECLIA statement. Review completion, actual dates and accreditation scope remain enquiry items; no earlier review approval is transferred to these new texts.
