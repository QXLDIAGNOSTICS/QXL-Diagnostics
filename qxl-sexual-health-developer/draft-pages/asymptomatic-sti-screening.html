STI Screening Without Symptoms – Testing After a New Partner

Possible HIV exposure within 72 hours? Seek urgent medical assessment for PEP now; do not wait for a laboratory test. Severe pelvic or testicular pain, heavy bleeding, breathing difficulty or sexual assault may need immediate medical care. Read urgent-care guidance.

Quick clinical answer

Many STIs can be asymptomatic. Screening should be based on individual risk, anatomy, exposure sites, age, pregnancy and previous diagnosis, not a fixed large panel for everyone.

What to do next

A new partner, multiple partners or a partner with a diagnosed infection can prompt evaluation. Discuss the need for repeat screening, vaccination and prevention with a clinician.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need tests if I feel healthy?

Possibly. Symptoms are not required for infection; a clinician can select screening based on risk.

Can I screen before a new relationship?

Yes. Couples can discuss testing, relevant windows and vaccination.

Should I order every PCR target including Ureaplasma?

Not routinely. Many organisms and tests are inappropriate for asymptomatic screening.
