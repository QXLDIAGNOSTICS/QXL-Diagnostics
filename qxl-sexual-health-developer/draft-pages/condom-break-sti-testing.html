Condom Broke or Slipped During Sex – STI Testing and Urgent Advice

Possible HIV exposure within 72 hours? Seek urgent medical assessment for PEP now; do not wait for a laboratory test. Severe pelvic or testicular pain, heavy bleeding, breathing difficulty or sexual assault may need immediate medical care. Read urgent-care guidance.

Quick clinical answer

A condom breaking, slipping, leaking or being removed during sex may expose mucosal surfaces to infection. Ejaculation is not required for all STI transmission.

What to do next

Consider prompt clinician assessment for HIV PEP and hepatitis B prevention, depending on exposure and vaccination. Pregnancy prevention may also be time-sensitive. Do not wait for symptoms.

Frequently asked questions

What if the condom broke but there was no ejaculation?

Risk is not zero for every STI; discuss the actual contact and partner status with a clinician.

Is PEP needed after every condom break?

No. A clinician evaluates HIV risk and timing; if indicated, PEP should start as soon as possible within 72 hours.

Can I take an STI test the next day?

Baseline testing can help, but a negative result may not detect the new exposure. Follow-up may be needed.
