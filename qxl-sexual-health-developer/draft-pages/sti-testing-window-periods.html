STI Testing Window Periods – When to Test After Sexual Exposure

Possible HIV exposure within 72 hours? Seek urgent medical assessment for PEP now; do not wait for a laboratory test. Severe pelvic or testicular pain, heavy bleeding, breathing difficulty or sexual assault may need immediate medical care. Read urgent-care guidance.

Quick clinical answer

There is no single window period for all STIs. Symptoms, specimen type, test method, time since exposure and medicines affect the plan. HIV laboratory antigen/antibody tests using venous blood usually detect infection 18–45 days after exposure in the absence of antiretroviral medication.

What to do next

A baseline test may be useful immediately. For symptoms, seek assessment now rather than waiting for an arbitrary interval. Follow-up testing is pathogen- and exposure-specific. Tests months or years later may detect current infection or lasting markers but cannot necessarily reconstruct a cleared infection.

Frequently asked questions

Can I test after 24 hours, 3 days or a week?

Yes for baseline assessment or symptoms, but early negative results may require follow-up.

Can I test at 2 weeks or one month?

Yes. A clinician selects molecular and serological tests and repeat timing; no universal panel is definitive at these times.

Is testing useful at 3, 6 or 8 months or one year?

Yes, depending on exposure. Current infection and relevant serological markers can be assessed; previous cleared infections may not be detectable.

What is the difference between incubation and window period?

Incubation is time until symptoms; the diagnostic window is time until a test can detect infection.
