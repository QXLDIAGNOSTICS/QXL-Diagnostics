Fourth-Generation HIV Testing After Exposure in Bangalore

Possible HIV exposure within 72 hours? Seek urgent medical assessment for PEP now; do not wait for a laboratory test. Severe pelvic or testicular pain, heavy bleeding, breathing difficulty or sexual assault may need immediate medical care. Read urgent-care guidance.

Quick clinical answer

A laboratory HIV-1/2 fourth-generation antigen/antibody test detects HIV-1 p24 antigen and antibodies to HIV-1/2. In people not taking antiretrovirals, venous laboratory Ag/Ab testing usually detects infection within 18–45 days; HIV NAT usually within 10–33 days.

What to do next

A reactive screening result requires supplemental testing under the applicable diagnostic algorithm. PEP or PrEP can affect detection and follow-up. No HIV test detects infection immediately; do not use an isolated index value, symptoms, CBC or a percentage calculator to diagnose HIV.

Frequently asked questions

Is HIV testing at 28, 30, 42 or 45 days reliable?

Interpret the result against the specific assay and exposure. If testing before the end of its window, follow-up may be indicated.

What about HIV test after 90 days, six months or a year?

A negative result after the applicable window without a new exposure is reassuring; antiretroviral use can change follow-up.

Can I have HIV without symptoms?

Yes. Symptoms are not a reliable way to diagnose or rule out HIV.

Does a reactive HIV screening test establish a diagnosis?

No. Supplemental testing and counselling are required.

What is HIV RNA PCR versus fourth-generation ECLIA?

NAT detects viral nucleic acid; Ag/Ab detects antigen and antibodies. The clinician selects the appropriate test.
