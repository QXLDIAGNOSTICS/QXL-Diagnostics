Persistent Urethritis and Mycoplasma genitalium Testing

Possible HIV exposure within 72 hours? Seek urgent medical assessment for PEP now; do not wait for a laboratory test. Severe pelvic or testicular pain, heavy bleeding, breathing difficulty or sexual assault may need immediate medical care. Read urgent-care guidance.

Quick clinical answer

Persistent or recurrent urethritis or cervicitis after initial evaluation may warrant Mycoplasma genitalium NAAT or other targeted testing, as clinically indicated.

What to do next

Routine screening for asymptomatic Mycoplasma genitalium is not recommended; routine Ureaplasma or Mycoplasma hominis testing is generally not appropriate. Assess reinfection, treatment history and non-infectious causes.

Frequently asked questions

Should I order Mycoplasma testing after every sexual exposure?

No. It is used in selected clinical scenarios.

What if CT/NG are negative but discharge continues?

Seek clinical assessment for other organisms, inflammation and non-infectious causes.

Does detecting Ureaplasma prove disease?

No. Detection may represent colonization and does not necessarily require treatment.
