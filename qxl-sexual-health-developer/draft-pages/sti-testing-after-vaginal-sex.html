STI Testing After Vaginal Sex – Blood, Urine and Genital Swabs

Possible HIV exposure within 72 hours? Seek urgent medical assessment for PEP now; do not wait for a laboratory test. Severe pelvic or testicular pain, heavy bleeding, breathing difficulty or sexual assault may need immediate medical care. Read urgent-care guidance.

Quick clinical answer

After vaginal intercourse, the appropriate specimens depend on anatomy and contact. A first-catch urine specimen or genital swab can be suitable for gonorrhoea/chlamydia NAAT; vaginal swabs are often preferred for vaginal/cervical screening.

What to do next

Consider HIV, syphilis and hepatitis tests based on risk, and pregnancy assessment if relevant. A normal urine routine report does not rule out an STI.

Frequently asked questions

Can infection occur without ejaculation?

Yes, depending on the infection and contact.

Do women need a urine test or vaginal swab?

The appropriate specimen depends on the assay and anatomy; a vaginal swab is often preferred for CT/NG screening.

Can I have an infection with no symptoms?

Yes. Discuss exposure-based screening.
