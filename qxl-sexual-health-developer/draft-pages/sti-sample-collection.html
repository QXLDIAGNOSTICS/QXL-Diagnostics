STI Specimen Collection – Blood, First-Catch Urine, Throat and Genital Swabs

Possible HIV exposure within 72 hours? Seek urgent medical assessment for PEP now; do not wait for a laboratory test. Severe pelvic or testicular pain, heavy bleeding, breathing difficulty or sexual assault may need immediate medical care. Read urgent-care guidance.

Quick clinical answer

The correct specimen varies with the investigation and the anatomical site. Follow the laboratory’s kit-specific instructions; a urine specimen intended for routine culture is not automatically appropriate for STI NAAT.

What to do next

Many serology tests do not require fasting. Confirm urine volume, time since urination, swab medium and transport. Some swabs require a trained clinician; self-collection is appropriate only where the assay and workflow support it.

Frequently asked questions

Can I eat or drink before STI blood tests?

Fasting is generally unnecessary for the tests discussed, unless another selected investigation requires it.

Can I use first-morning or midstream urine?

Follow the exact test instruction; first-catch urine means the initial urine stream, not necessarily first morning.

Can I collect a swab at home?

Only if the specific assay and laboratory workflow support it.

Does mouthwash, menstruation or recent intercourse affect collection?

Requirements vary by assay. Confirm before collecting and disclose relevant medications or symptoms.
