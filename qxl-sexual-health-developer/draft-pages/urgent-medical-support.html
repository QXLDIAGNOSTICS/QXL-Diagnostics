Urgent Medical Help After Sexual Exposure or Sexual Assault

Possible HIV exposure within 72 hours? Seek urgent medical assessment for PEP now; do not wait for a laboratory test. Severe pelvic or testicular pain, heavy bleeding, breathing difficulty or sexual assault may need immediate medical care. Read urgent-care guidance.

Quick clinical answer

Possible HIV exposure within 72 hours, recent sexual assault, severe pelvic or testicular pain, heavy bleeding or serious systemic symptoms need prompt medical attention. A laboratory booking must never delay care.

What to do next

Seek a qualified emergency clinician. After non-consensual contact, medical support, prevention, forensic options, consent and counselling can be discussed without first purchasing a test. If immediate safety is at risk, contact local emergency services.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to wait for a laboratory test before seeking help?

No. Seek care now; the clinician can arrange testing and prevention.

Can emergency contraception be considered after assault?

Yes, when relevant and time-sensitive; a qualified clinician can discuss options.

Can I get care without sharing my history publicly?

Yes. Discuss confidentiality and consent privately with the medical team.
