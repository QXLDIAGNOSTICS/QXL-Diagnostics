Fever, Rash, Swollen Glands or HIV Anxiety After Sex

Possible HIV exposure within 72 hours? Seek urgent medical assessment for PEP now; do not wait for a laboratory test. Severe pelvic or testicular pain, heavy bleeding, breathing difficulty or sexual assault may need immediate medical care. Read urgent-care guidance.

Quick clinical answer

Fever, fatigue, rash, ulcers, swollen glands, night sweats and body pain are nonspecific. HIV may be present without symptoms; symptoms cannot confirm or exclude infection.

What to do next

Use an appropriate test and its window period. Repeated testing after an adequate negative result may not help persistent anxiety. Seek medical and counselling support rather than interpreting every symptom or test index as evidence of HIV.

Frequently asked questions

Can anxiety cause physical symptoms?

Yes, but persistent or severe symptoms should be clinically assessed.

Can a normal CBC rule out HIV?

No. HIV-specific testing is necessary when indicated.

All my HIV tests are negative but I am afraid. What next?

Review the assay, exposure timing, medications and new exposures with a clinician; consider counselling if uncertainty persists.
