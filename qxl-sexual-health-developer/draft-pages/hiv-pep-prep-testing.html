HIV PEP and PrEP – Urgent Care, Baseline and Follow-Up Testing

Possible HIV exposure within 72 hours? Seek urgent medical assessment for PEP now; do not wait for a laboratory test. Severe pelvic or testicular pain, heavy bleeding, breathing difficulty or sexual assault may need immediate medical care. Read urgent-care guidance.

Quick clinical answer

PEP is emergency HIV prevention after a potential exposure; seek medical assessment as soon as possible and within 72 hours. PrEP is prevention for people with ongoing risk. Neither is a substitute for prevention or testing for other STIs.

What to do next

A clinician determines eligibility, prescribes medication, and orders appropriate baseline and follow-up HIV, hepatitis, renal and other tests. Do not delay PEP for laboratory results or self-medicate. If the 72-hour window has passed, obtain medical advice about testing and ongoing prevention.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I get PEP urgently?

Visit an emergency department, qualified clinician or HIV service immediately. A routine laboratory appointment is not a substitute.

Can PEP prevent gonorrhoea or syphilis?

No. HIV PEP is directed at HIV.

When do I test after PEP or while taking PrEP?

Follow the clinician’s current protocol; HIV Ag/Ab and NAT may be indicated. Standard untreated windows cannot simply be applied.
